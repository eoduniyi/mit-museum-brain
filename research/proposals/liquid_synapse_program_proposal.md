## 1. Executive Summary

**Liquid Synapse** is a two-part public program combining a hands-on tactile workshop with a live audiovisual concert and lecture-demonstration at the MIT Museum. The program demystifies neurotechnology and artificial intelligence by reframing cognitive processes through the intuitive, physical paradigm of **modular hardware synthesis** and **continuous-time "Liquid Learning"**.

Rather than treating the brain as a black box or a discrete, static computer, *Liquid Synapse* embodies the nervous system as a fluid, continuous dynamical medium. Museum visitors are invited to step beyond the screen:
1. **Afternoon Public Masterclass & Build Lab** (MIT Museum Maker Hub): Participants work with custom software/hardware consoles modeled after analog Eurorack synthesizers and studio VST instruments. They patch biometric control voltages (CV)—including micro-EEG oscillations, attention entropy, and ocular saccades—directly into continuous neural Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs), listening to cognition as resonant acoustic frequencies.
2. **Evening Live Concert & Keynote Demonstration** (The Lee Family Exchange): Utilizing the monumental two-story digital screen and spatial audio array, the instruments built and calibrated during the workshop are linked into a communal neural matrix, creating a real-time, room-scale performance of collective cognitive adaptation.

## 2. Thematic Alignment: The Brain as an Open Instrument

The MIT Museum’s **BRAIN** theme investigates the brain as an internal command center shaped by physiology, external environmental stimuli, and emergent technologies. *Liquid Synapse* tackles three central pillars of this theme:

### 1. From Discrete Tokens to Continuous Fluids (Liquid Learning)
Mainstream discourse on AI and the brain is often dominated by discrete transformer architectures and static digital logic. However, biological neurons are fundamentally continuous-time physical systems governed by non-linear fluid dynamics, ion fluxes, and variable membrane time-constants. Grounded in research on **Liquid Neural Networks (LNNs)**, *Liquid Synapse* invites the public to hear and visualize how learning occurs when time is treated as a continuous flow rather than chopped into frozen intervals.

### 2. The Tactile Emulation of the Non-Conscious
Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) often suffer from alienation—users stare at cryptic graphs or disconnect from the somatic reality of their nervous systems. Drawing on high-end analog synthesizer aesthetics (anodized aluminum panels, weighted tactile knobs, precision VU meters, and Swiss constructivist calibration scales), the program translates invisible neuro-electrical impulses into tangible physical interactions that anyone can touch, shape, and hear.

### 3. Neurodiversity, Resonance, and Collective States
By mapping neural rhythms to acoustic harmony and organic phase portraits, the program creates an inclusive, non-evaluative space. Cognitive states such as daydreaming, focus, auditory reaction, and stillness are not categorized as "good" or "bad," but as unique acoustic timbres in a collective chamber ensemble.

## 3. Program Components & Structure

The proposed program is designed as a Saturday/Sunday feature with two integrated phases:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 1: THE TACTILE LAB (Afternoon, 2.5 Hours)             │
│ Venue: MIT Museum Maker Hub / Learning Labs                 │
│ Capacity: 25–35 registered participants                     │
│ Activity: Patching bio-modular software synthesizers,       │
│           calibrating neural ODEs, acoustic sound design.   │
└──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                               │
                               ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 2: THE SYMPOSIUM & CONCERT (Evening, 75 Minutes)      │
│ Venue: The Lee Family Exchange (Two-Story Atrium)           │
│ Capacity: 150–250 public attendees                          │
│ Activity: Keynote lecture, multi-channel spatial audio,     │
│           live 2-story projection of fluid neural manifolds.│
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Phase 1: Hands-On Masterclass — "Patching the Mind"
- **Target Audience:** MIT students, local artists, musicians, high school educators, and curious museum visitors (no prior coding or neuroscience background required).
- **Format:** Guided lab session at communal worktables.
- **Curriculum:**
  - *Module 01 (30 min): Principles of Neural Dynamics.* Introduction to how biological neurons generate rhythmic field potentials and how Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) equations model these processes.
  - *Module 02 (45 min): The Bio-Modular Console.* Participants launch the open-source *Liquid Synapse* tactile software environment. They learn how to route control voltages, adjust decay constants ($\tau$), and connect modulation sources (simulated or live EEG, ocular tracking, and mouse/touch telemetry).
  - *Module 03 (45 min): Sound & Timbre Sculpting.* Mapping cognitive state-spaces to granular synthesizers, FM operators, and physical modeling acoustic strings.
  - *Module 04 (30 min): Rehearsal & Parameter Patching.* Calibrating individual instruments to join the evening ensemble.

### Phase 2: Live Performance & Keynote — "The Fluid Mind"
- **Venue:** Yuchun (1989) and Agustina Lee Family Exchange.
- **Visuals:** The two-story LED screen displays a Swiss constructivist scientific telemetry dashboard and real-time organic phase portraits showing the trajectory of the neural manifold in high dimension.
- **Acoustics:** Quadraphonic / spatial sound array projecting pure analog sine sub-tones, evolving harmonic choral textures, and responsive micro-rhythms generated by the participant ensemble.
- **Structure:**
  - *Opening Lecture (15 min):* Erick Oduniyi on the frontier of continuous learning, physical computing, and the poetic interface between brain and machine.
  - *The Performance (40 min):* Live improvisational performance where participant consoles interact with ambient sound from the audience, modulating the visual and sonic architecture of the museum atrium.
  - *Audience Q&A & Open Console (20 min):* General museum visitors are invited up to the central performance console to turn knobs, adjust patch cables, and experience the instrument firsthand.

## 4. Technical Infrastructure & Spatial Setup

### Space 1: The Lee Family Exchange
- **Display:** Museum's two-story media wall (direct HDMI/DisplayPort feed at native resolution from lead performance workstation).
- **Audio:** Museum PA system or 4-point surround sound reinforcement; line inputs for master stereo audio console.
- **Staging:** Sleek center table with tabletop Eurorack modular gear, tactile hardware MIDI/CV controllers, and monitoring displays.
- **Seating:** Amphitheater / tiered stair seating for public audience.

### Space 2: The Maker Hub / Learning Labs
- **Furniture:** 6–8 workbenches with power strips.
- **Participant Stations:** Laptops (BYOD or MIT Museum loaner Chromebooks/Macs running modern browsers via WebAudio/WebGL).
- **Biometric Demonstration Stations:** 2–3 research-grade consumer EEG headsets (e.g. OpenBCI / Muse 2) and eye-tracking webcams provided by project lead for live signal routing.

## 6. Lead Facilitator Biography & Research Background

**Erick Oduniyi** is a researcher, engineer, and designer who studied at the **MIT Media Lab** within the **Viral Communications Group**. His research investigates **Liquid Learning**—computational architectures that model continuous-time dynamics, fluid state transitions, and biological adaptability. 

Oduniyi specializes in the design of functional, aesthetic software interfaces that emulate the somatic qualities of physical audio hardware and precision laboratory instrumentation. His work spans computational biology, ocular morphology, tactile VST instruments, and Swiss constructivist information design. In Fall 2026, he created the *Chromatic Spectral Bands & Cryogenic Retinal Matrix* architecture for the inaugural MIT Future Fest. His practice bridges rigorous mathematical modeling with accessible, deeply tactile public experiences.
