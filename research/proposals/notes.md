# Notes for BRAIN and Future Arts
## What part of the process do you want to present?

I feel like at the end of the day it's about both the process and the result. Both can be artifacts. One might reinforce the other. The practice may be inseparable. Still, as a cognitive creature, you are likely to be stimulated faster not because of the process that is described, but because the artwork itself is visceral. There are many cases where the art work isn't as stimulating until you understand the process behind it. But I love this idea that from the artwork you can know the process as in the reconstruction of the phenomena is clear.  

But also, I think many artists don't disclose their process. So, I realize we have an opportunity to extend provenance to the level of artistic process as opposed to artistic market. However, that reveals our obsession with surveillance. You know we could just record the process we make and then apply some art-activity-annotator model to distill the creative steps, but I don't think that's an output that is particularly meaningful in 2026. And that's okay; it could still be a useful idea, and I'm sure many people are already doing some form of this, or it is an implicit part of some practices (e.g., multi-stream setups coordinated through TouchDesigner).

Again, I think it's interesting to consider that provenance typically isn't captured at the level of the artist's changes. I mean, those were probably categorized (logged), or by the progression of the prototypes or preliminary works, one might be able to reconstruct the final output, as it is often the case where we'll find a piece from an artist that wasn't the "main" or even completed piece, e.g., Georgia O'keeffe's [insert your favorite underrated piece]. And then what does it mean for a piece to be completed? When does the artist say this is done? 

Provenance isn't about the artist's intent or desire to present the process or main work. That's a design choice that museums and those who archive the art must make. Do we value the final piece more than the process or the insights gained during the process?

## Autonomous, Generative, and Coordinated Art Practices
Take the autonomy out and you basically *prompt* [insert your favorite generative model] into completion. A completed work asks, "what else?". Put the autonomy back in, and now the model might start asking it's own questions. Depending on the day or model, it might start asking you questions in plain English. Take the artist (human) out, and now you have nature (bunch of rules that barely make sense). That is, natural and digital communication. Like other animals, but in our languages, we call this expression. One may have an expression but not a process to enact it in a way some cultures call art. But it always is, because the act of expressing is creation. It may not be what you wanted to see, hear, or feel, but from an imagination it was instrumented in reality and we can tune to it and wonder, "how did we get here?"
